# 4. Conclusions

Literature-trained models of hydrothermal conversion predict runs from studies they have seen far better than runs from new studies: random splitting overstated accuracy about two-fold across learner families, and most of the remaining error was a study-level offset that recorded work-up and yield-basis descriptors did not explain. Conformal intervals calibrated on random rows were over-confident for new studies, whereas calibrating at the level of studies restored near-nominal coverage, and a few runs from the new study combined through empirical-Bayes shrinkage narrowed both errors and intervals. Study-grouped validation, study-calibrated intervals and local anchoring runs are recommended as minimum practice for literature-trained bioresource surrogates.
