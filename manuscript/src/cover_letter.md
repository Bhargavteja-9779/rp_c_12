**Submission of a Full Length Article: "Machine-learning models of hydrothermal biomass conversion trained on literature data: transfer to unseen studies and study-calibrated prediction intervals"**

Dear Editor,

We submit the above manuscript for consideration as a Full Length Article in *Bioresource Technology*.

Machine-learning surrogates of hydrothermal liquefaction and carbonization trained on literature-compiled data are now common in the journal, but they are almost always validated by splitting experimental runs at random. Using the largest public curated hydrothermal database (3,693 runs, 188 studies) and a set of 38 studies locked away before any modelling, we show that random splitting overstates the accuracy of such models for new studies about two-fold across 12 learner families; that most of the remaining error is a study-level offset; that recorded work-up and yield-basis descriptors do not explain it; and that conformal prediction intervals calibrated on random rows under-cover new studies (coverage {{biooil_cov_RC}} instead of 0.90 for bio-oil yield). We propose a model-agnostic procedure that calibrates intervals at the level of studies and adapts to a new study from a few local runs with empirical-Bayes shrinkage, restoring near-nominal coverage.

The work builds directly on recent contributions in this journal — in particular the physically admissible HTL surrogates of Elfetni et al. (2027, doi:10.1016/j.biortech.2026.135587), which documented inter-study heterogeneity — and addresses questions those studies left open: the size and source of the heterogeneity and the validity of uncertainty estimates for new studies. We believe the results are of direct practical relevance to the journal's readers who build or use data-driven bioresource process models.

All analyses are reproducible from public CC-BY data with the supplied code; every number in the manuscript is generated programmatically from the result files. The analysis protocol and locked test set were committed before modelling, and all deviations are reported.

We confirm that this manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors have approved the manuscript and agree with its submission. The study used only published, openly licensed data and involved no human participants or animals; no ethical approval was required. The authors declare no competing interests.
