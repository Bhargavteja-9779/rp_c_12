**Submission of a Full Length Article: "Process effects versus laboratory effects in hydrothermal liquefaction and carbonization of lignocellulosic biomass: a within-study meta-analysis"**

Dear Editor,

We submit the above manuscript for consideration as a Full Length Article in *Bioresource Technology*.

Hydrothermal liquefaction and carbonization have been studied in hundreds of experimental papers, many of them in this journal, yet there is no quantitative synthesis of how much temperature, residence time, catalysts, co-solvents and solid loading actually change product yields — and how consistently. Narrative reviews summarise trends qualitatively, and the machine-learning models now common in the field pool experiments from many laboratories, mixing laboratory differences with process effects.

Our manuscript reports, to our knowledge, the first meta-analysis of hydrothermal liquefaction and carbonization process effects. Using the largest public curated database (3,693 experiments, 188 studies), we estimated every effect only from comparisons within a study — runs that differ in a single factor — so that laboratory-specific offsets cancel, and synthesised {{ma_n_contrasts}} such comparisons from {{ma_n_studies}} studies with random-effects models under a protocol registered before any effect was computed. Key findings for the journal's readers:

- Within studies, bio-oil yield rises by about {{m_biooil_T_250_300}} wt% per 10 °C up to 300 °C and not beyond; catalysts raise bio-oil yield by {{m_biooil_cat_alkali_carbonate}}–{{m_biooil_cat_metal_supported}} wt% and energy recovery by {{m_energy_biooil_cat_alkali_hydroxide}}–{{m_energy_biooil_cat_metal_supported}} percentage points by shifting mass from the solid to the liquid products.
- Effects vary strongly between laboratories (median I² {{ma_I2_median}}); we report prediction intervals and the probability that a change helps in a new laboratory.
- The laboratory itself matters more than any single process change: unexplained between-study offsets ({{biooil_tau}}–{{lab_mm_biooil_tau}} wt% for bio-oil) exceed every catalyst effect.
- Pooled machine-learning models reproduce average effects but hide their variability, and SHAP importance of feedstock descriptors partly reflects laboratory differences.
- Temperature effects replicated in 38 studies held out from the start; a pre-registered test showed that average effects do not predict individual laboratories better than assuming no change, which we report transparently.

These results give experimentalists quantitative, uncertainty-aware priors for planning hydrothermal experiments and give authors of data-driven studies a benchmark for interpreting their models. The work uses the same open database as Elfetni et al. (2027, doi:10.1016/j.biortech.2026.135587), which developed physically admissible machine-learning surrogates; none of the present authors is an author of that work, and our question — within-study process effects and between-laboratory heterogeneity — is different.

All data are openly available (RADAR4Chem, doi:10.22000/8bnbhqccf4aq7kea; 4TU.ResearchData, doi:10.4121/834c3f22-f46a-4808-a0fe-46298be6a133.v1); the code and per-contrast data are available from the corresponding author on request. No new experiments were performed.
