# Abstract

Hundreds of studies have examined how temperature, residence time, catalysts and solvents affect hydrothermal liquefaction (HTL) and carbonization (HTC), yet their results are pooled qualitatively or by machine-learning models that mix laboratory differences with process effects. This study reports, to our knowledge, the first quantitative meta-analysis of HTL/HTC process effects. From a curated database of 3,693 experiments on mainly lignocellulosic biomass, {{ma_n_contrasts}} within-study comparisons from {{ma_n_studies}} development studies — runs of the same study that differ in one factor only — were synthesised with random-effects models under a protocol registered beforehand; 38 further studies were held out. Raising the temperature by 10 °C within 250–300 °C increased bio-oil yield by {{m_biooil_T_250_300}} wt% ({{m_biooil_T_250_300_k}} studies) and decreased solid yield by {{m_solid_T_250_300_abs}} wt%. Catalysts raised bio-oil yield by {{m_biooil_cat_alkali_carbonate}} (alkali carbonates) to {{m_biooil_cat_metal_supported}} wt% (metal/supported catalysts), lowered solid yield by {{m_solid_cat_alkali_hydroxide_abs}}–{{m_solid_cat_metal_supported_abs}} wt% and raised energy recovery to bio-oil by {{m_energy_biooil_cat_alkali_hydroxide}}–{{m_energy_biooil_cat_metal_supported}} percentage points. Between-laboratory heterogeneity was large (median I² {{ma_I2_median}}): catalysts are expected to increase bio-oil yield in only {{m_biooil_cat_alkali_hydroxide_pbpct}}–{{m_biooil_cat_metal_supported_pbpct}} of new laboratories, and laboratory offsets (SD {{biooil_tau}}–{{lab_mm_biooil_tau}} wt%) exceeded every catalyst effect. A pooled machine-learning model reproduced the mean effects (Spearman ρ = {{px_rho}}) but not their spread, and in held-out studies the mean effects predicted individual outcomes no better than assuming no change. Process effects in HTL/HTC are real but laboratory-specific; data-driven guidance should report prediction intervals and be confirmed locally.
