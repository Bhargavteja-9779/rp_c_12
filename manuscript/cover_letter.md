**Submission of a Full Length Article: "From literature to laboratory: an uncertainty-aware framework for transferring machine-learning models of hydrothermal biomass conversion and planning first experiments"**

Dear Editor,

We submit the above manuscript for consideration as a Full Length Article in *Bioresource Technology*.

Machine-learning models of hydrothermal liquefaction and carbonization trained on literature data are now common in the journal, but they are almost always validated by splitting experimental runs at random, although they are meant to help laboratories whose studies they have never seen. Our manuscript evaluates this deployment problem directly, for bio-oil and solid yields, using the public curated hydrothermal database (3,693 runs, 188 studies), 38 studies locked away before any modelling, an independent hydrochar database and a protocol fixed in advance.

The main findings are practical. Random splitting overstated accuracy about two-fold, and most of the error for a new study was a study-level offset that reported work-up descriptors did not explain. Conformal intervals calibrated across studies covered 0.82–0.91 of new-study runs, against 0.46–0.61 for intervals calibrated on random runs. In a retrospective simulated deployment, three runs placed by a simple screening design and combined with the literature model reduced the median per-study error by 1.6–6.4 wt% and outperformed the local runs alone for unseen development studies — but not for the locked bio-oil studies, a negative result we report together with an applicability-domain threshold that did not anticipate it. These results are condensed into a workflow (STRATA-Lab), a capability matrix and a minimum validation checklist intended for authors and reviewers of literature-trained bioresource models.

The work uses the same open database as a recent article in this journal (Elfetni et al., 2027, doi:10.1016/j.biortech.2026.135587) and a related preprint by the same group (doi:10.2139/ssrn.6816225). None of the present authors is an author of either work. The manuscript contains a direct comparison (Table 1): those studies address physically admissible multi-phase surrogates and energy/carbon routing, whereas ours addresses coverage of intervals for unseen studies, literature-anchored versus local-only prediction after a few designed runs, decision support and held-out evaluation; aqueous and gas phases are explicitly outside our scope.

All data analysed are openly available (RADAR4Chem, doi:10.22000/8bnbhqccf4aq7kea; 4TU.ResearchData, doi:10.4121/834c3f22-f46a-4808-a0fe-46298be6a133.v1). The analysis code, locked study list and result files are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request; every number in the manuscript is generated programmatically from the result files. The validation is retrospective, and no new experiments were performed; this is stated in the abstract, the methods and the limitations.

We confirm that this manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors have approved the manuscript and agree with its submission. The study used only published, openly licensed data and involved no human participants or animals.
